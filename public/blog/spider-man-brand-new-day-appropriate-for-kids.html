Is Spider-Man: Brand New Day Appropriate for Kids?

🍿 WatchWithKids July 27, 2026 6 min read

Spider-Man: Brand New Day swings into theaters July 31, 2026. Here's what's confirmed about its content so far — and an honest note on what isn't confirmed yet, since the film hasn't been fully reviewed by third-party critics as of this writing.

A note on timing

This film releases July 31, 2026. As of publishing, the official MPAA rating and reasoning are confirmed, and pre-release guidance from several family-media outlets agrees on the broad strokes below. Full post-screening reviews — including Common Sense Media's complete write-up — are expected on release day. We'll be conservative about anything not yet confirmed.

Quick verdict

Spider-Man: Brand New Day is officially rated PG-13 by the MPAA for "sequences of action/violence and some language." Directed by Destin Daniel Cretton, it's the fourth MCU Spider-Man film starring Tom Holland, continuing directly from 2021's Spider-Man: No Way Home.

Pre-release guidance from multiple family-media outlets converges on a similar recommendation: most kids around 10 and up should handle it fine, ideally with a parent nearby, while younger or more sensitive children may want to wait. That's consistent with a standard MCU PG-13 outing — not a horror-adjacent film, but also not the more kid-accessible register of, say, Homecoming.

Why it's rated PG-13

The official MPAA reasoning is action/violence and some language. Pre-release parent guides — working from the confirmed rating, the trailers, and released marketing material — describe what to expect in more detail:

Violence
Frequent superhero combat

Guides describe recurring hand-to-hand combat, chases, and large-scale action sequences typical of the MCU, with some sources noting the tone reads slightly darker than earlier Holland-era Spider-Man films — partly attributed to the inclusion of the Punisher (Jon Bernthal), a character established elsewhere in the MCU as considerably harder-edged than Spider-Man's usual rogues' gallery.

Language
Some, described as moderate

Pre-release guides describe the language as present but not pervasive — consistent with a standard MCU PG-13, well short of anything approaching an R-rated register.

Themes
Isolation and identity

The core premise — Peter Parker living alone after a spell erased the world's memory of his identity — is emotionally heavier than a typical team-up film. Expect a more introspective, lonelier Peter than previous entries.

Other
No sexual content

Pre-release guides consistently note no sexual content or nudity. This is standard for the Holland-era Spider-Man films.

What the film is about

Following the ending of Spider-Man: No Way Home, a spell caused the entire world — including Peter's closest friends MJ (Zendaya) and Ned (Jacob Batalon) — to forget he exists. Brand New Day picks up with Peter navigating college, work, and his responsibilities as Spider-Man essentially alone, without the support network or advanced technology that defined his earlier films. The cast includes Zendaya, Jacob Batalon, Jon Bernthal, Mark Ruffalo, and Sadie Sink.

Multiple guides describe this as a deliberate tonal reset — less reliant on prior Avengers infrastructure, more grounded in Peter's personal isolation. Reviewers who've seen early marketing material suggest the film may actually be more accessible to newcomers than No Way Home was, since it isn't as dependent on multiverse continuity — though the emotional beats will land harder for viewers who know Peter's full history.

Age-by-age guidance

Under 10

Most pre-release guides suggest this age range should wait or watch cautiously alongside a parent. The combat sequences and the film's more isolated, melancholy tone are likely to land differently — and more heavily — than the friendlier register of Homecoming.

10–12

This is the range most guides converge on as reasonable with a parent present. Kids who've seen the previous Holland-era films and handled them well are likely candidates, though the added darkness from the Punisher's inclusion is worth knowing about going in.

13 and up

Squarely the film's intended audience. A standard MCU PG-13 outing at this age.

Our recommendation

If your child is younger than 10 or easily unsettled by intense action, consider waiting for the full reviews that land on release day (July 31) before committing to a theater trip — or wait for streaming, where you can preview scenes yourself first.

Frequently asked questions

Is Spider-Man: Brand New Day appropriate for kids?

It's rated PG-13 for action/violence and some language. Pre-release guidance suggests it's best suited to kids around 10 and up, ideally watched with a parent, given a somewhat darker tone than earlier Holland-era Spider-Man films.

What age rating is Spider-Man: Brand New Day?

The MPAA has officially rated the film PG-13 for sequences of action/violence and some language.

Is Spider-Man: Brand New Day darker than previous Spider-Man movies?

Several pre-release guides describe it as somewhat more mature in tone than earlier Tom Holland Spider-Man films, largely due to Peter's isolated circumstances and the inclusion of the Punisher, a notably harder-edged MCU character.

Do I need to see the other Spider-Man movies first?

It's recommended but may not be strictly necessary. Some pre-release guides suggest the film's fresh-start premise could make it more accessible to newcomers than No Way Home was, though the emotional impact will be stronger if you've seen Homecoming and No Way Home first.
