Best Movies for 7 Year Olds in 2026

MAY 2026 · 6 MIN READ · WATCHWITHKIDS

Seven is a genuinely exciting age for movie nights. They can handle real emotional stakes, follow complex plots, and appreciate genuine humour — but they're still young enough to be completely swept away by wonder and adventure. These 14 films hit that window perfectly.

We've sorted by mood so you can pick based on the evening you're having — a rainy Saturday afternoon calls for something different than a school-night wind-down. Each pick includes an honest note for the parents in the room.

⚡ Adventure — for when they have energy to burn

Seven-year-olds who've been sitting still all day at school need something that matches their energy. These deliver real excitement without anything too intense for this age.

🕷️

Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse (2018)

Ages 7–12 117 min Netflix

A Brooklyn teenager discovers he's not the only Spider-Man in the multiverse. The animation style is genuinely revolutionary — nothing before or since looks quite like it. A story about identity, belonging, and learning to believe in yourself that works on every level.

⭐ Parent note: One of the best films made in the last decade, not just for kids. Prepare to be completely absorbed.

🐉

How to Train Your Dragon (2010)

Ages 5–12 98 min Netflix

A young Viking who can't fight befriends the most feared dragon in the world. The flying sequences are breathtaking even 15 years later, and the central message about empathy and difference is handled with remarkable subtlety for a kids' film.

⭐ Parent note: Holds up beautifully on rewatch. The score alone is worth the runtime.

🌊

Moana (2016)

Ages 4–10 107 min Disney+

A Polynesian chief's daughter sails beyond the reef to save her island and restore the heart of Te Fiti. Visually stunning ocean sequences, a genuinely strong female lead who doesn't need a love interest, and songs that live in your head forever.

⭐ Parent note: "How Far I'll Go" will be performed at full volume in your living room. Repeatedly. Accept this.

🦁

Puss in Boots: The Last Wish (2022)

Ages 6–12 100 min Netflix

A swashbuckling cat facing the end of his nine lives goes on a quest for a wishing star. The animation is unlike anything in mainstream animated film — expressive, painterly, and exhilarating. The villain is genuinely iconic. Seven-year-olds absolutely love this one.

⭐ Parent note: Critically, this is the best animated film in years. Adults consistently rate it higher than the kids.

😄 Silly — for rainy days and weekend mornings

Seven-year-olds have highly sophisticated taste in comedy — they want clever wordplay, sharp timing, and slapstick in equal measure. These deliver on all three.

🧱

The LEGO Movie (2014)

Ages 5–12 100 min Any

An ordinary LEGO minifig is mistakenly identified as the most extraordinary person in the world. Relentlessly funny, cleverly constructed, and hiding a genuinely moving twist in the third act that nobody sees coming — especially not seven-year-olds.

⭐ Parent note: The ending is for the adults. It hits differently when you're a parent. You'll understand.

⚡

The Incredibles (2004)

Ages 5–12 115 min Disney+

A family of superheroes forced into hiding springs back into action when a new villain emerges. Pixar at its sharpest — it's simultaneously a brilliant superhero film, a comedy about suburban mediocrity, and a story about a marriage under pressure. Seven-year-olds see only the thrilling action.

⭐ Parent note: Edna Mode is one of the great comic characters in film history. She gets better every rewatch.

🤖

The Mitchells vs. the Machines (2021)

Ages 6–12 114 min Netflix

A chaotic family road trip gets interrupted by a robot apocalypse. From the producers of Spider-Verse, with the same inventive animation energy. Hilarious, fast-paced, and secretly a film about a father who struggles to understand his daughter — which lands beautifully.

⭐ Parent note: One of the most genuinely funny family films made in recent years. Adults laugh harder than the kids.

🎵 Musical — for kids who love to sing

Seven-year-olds are at the perfect age for musicals — old enough to follow the story, young enough to have zero inhibitions about singing along at full volume.

🎺

Coco (2017)

Ages 5–12 105 min Disney+

A young boy journeys to the Land of the Dead to uncover his family's secret history. Pixar's most emotionally ambitious film — tackles death, memory, and legacy through stunning colour, music, and storytelling. Seven-year-olds are riveted from the opening frame; parents are destroyed by the finale.

⭐ Parent note: The last 15 minutes are among the most moving sequences in any animated film. Keep tissues close.

🕯️

Encanto (2021)

Ages 4–10 99 min Disney+

A Colombian family where everyone has magical powers — except one girl. A Disney musical that's unusually honest about family pressure, sibling dynamics, and being overlooked. The Lin-Manuel Miranda songs are genuinely brilliant and "We Don't Talk About Bruno" will live in your household for months.

⭐ Parent note: One of the rare Disney films with no villain. Refreshingly honest about family dysfunction.

📚

Matilda the Musical (2022)

Ages 5–12 117 min Netflix

Roald Dahl's beloved story reimagined as a bold, defiant musical. A brilliant girl with terrible parents discovers she has extraordinary powers and uses them to fight back. The songs are exhilarating, the performances are extraordinary, and it champions reading and independent thinking throughout.

⭐ Parent note: Emma Thompson as Miss Trunchbull is one of the great villain performances in recent memory.

😌 Calm — for school nights and winding down

It's 7pm on a Tuesday. You need something beautiful and engaging that won't send them to bed wired. These are your best options.

🌿

My Neighbor Totoro (1988)

Ages 3–10 86 min Max

Two sisters discover magical forest creatures near their new home in rural Japan. Studio Ghibli's most purely gentle film — almost no conflict, just wonder. At 86 minutes it fits neatly into a school-night window and leaves everyone calm and happy.

⭐ Parent note: Many adults find this film genuinely calming to watch. A rare thing in children's entertainment.

🧹

Kiki's Delivery Service (1989)

Ages 5–12 103 min Max

A young witch moves to a charming coastal city and sets up a flying delivery service. A quietly empowering story about independence, self-doubt, and finding your way in a new place. Beautifully paced and visually lovely — perfect for a gentle evening watch.

⭐ Parent note: Miyazaki's most accessible film for children who are ready for something slightly more grown-up.

🎄

Klaus (2019)

Ages 4–10 96 min Netflix

A selfish young postman forms an unlikely friendship with a reclusive toymaker, accidentally inventing the legend of Santa Claus. Netflix's most gorgeous original film — hand-drawn animation that looks unlike anything in mainstream cinema and a story about kindness that earns every emotion it asks for.

⭐ Parent note: The ending will make you cry without warning. Seven-year-olds find it completely magical.

Quick reference — by mood

  • Adventure: Spider-Verse, How to Train Your Dragon, Moana, Puss in Boots
  • Silly: The LEGO Movie, The Incredibles, Mitchells vs. Machines
  • Musical: Coco, Encanto, Matilda the Musical
  • Calm: My Neighbor Totoro, Kiki's Delivery Service, Klaus
  • Under 100 min: Totoro (86), Puss in Boots (100), Encanto (99), Klaus (96)

Tips for movie night with a 7-year-old

They're ready for real emotional stakes. Seven-year-olds can handle sadness, loss, and moral complexity in a way younger children can't. Films like Coco and Klaus that deal with death and memory are appropriate — and often prompt the best conversations you'll have.

Match the length to your window. A 7pm start with an 8:30 bedtime gives you roughly 90 minutes. Coco (105 min) and Spider-Verse (117 min) run long for a school night — save those for weekends. Klaus (96 min) and My Neighbor Totoro (86 min) are your best school-night options.

Let them pick from a shortlist. Rather than asking "what do you want to watch?" — which leads to 20 minutes of indecision — offer two or three options. "Coco or The Incredibles?" is a decision a seven-year-old can make in under five seconds.

Stay for the credits. Seven-year-olds who've just seen something they love want to sit in it for a moment before the lights go up. The habit of staying through the credits is a good one — and Pixar almost always hides something worth seeing in them.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best movie for a 7-year-old in 2026?

For most seven-year-olds, Puss in Boots: The Last Wish and Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse are the most universally loved right now — both have outstanding animation, strong stories, and work brilliantly for parents watching alongside. For something calmer, Klaus on Netflix is the standout pick.

What movies are appropriate for a 7-year-old?

G and PG rated films are generally appropriate for age 7, though content varies significantly within the PG category. All 14 films on this list have been chosen specifically for seven-year-olds. The ones with the most emotional intensity — Coco and Spider-Verse — are still very appropriate but may prompt questions about death and loss worth being ready for.

What are the best Disney movies for 7-year-olds?

Moana, Encanto, and The Incredibles are the strongest Disney picks for this age group. All three are on Disney+ and work beautifully for seven-year-olds. Coco (Pixar/Disney) is also exceptional — arguably the best of the group for emotional depth.

What are the best movies for a 7-year-old who gets scared easily?

My Neighbor Totoro is the safest pick on this list — almost no conflict or danger whatsoever. Klaus and Kiki's Delivery Service are also gentle. Avoid Spider-Verse and Puss in Boots for children who are sensitive to action sequences or intense villains.

How long should a movie be for a 7-year-old?

Most seven-year-olds can comfortably watch films up to around 110 minutes, especially on weekends. For school nights, aim for 100 minutes or under to allow for bed and wind-down time. Use the WatchWithKids length filter to find films that fit your specific window.
