Is Toy Story 5 Appropriate for Kids?

🍿 WatchWithKids July 27, 2026 6 min read

Toy Story 5 has been in theaters since June 19, 2026, and takes the franchise somewhere new: the toys' real rival this time isn't another toy, it's a tablet. Here's what's actually in it, verified against multiple parent-review sources.

Quick verdict

Toy Story 5 is rated PG by the MPAA for "some thematic elements and rude humor," and runs 102 minutes. Multiple reviewers describe it as a genuinely family-friendly film with no graphic violence, no sexual content, and no strong profanity — the PG comes almost entirely from its emotional themes around technology, screen addiction, and being replaced, rather than from anything visually intense.

Common Sense Media rates it appropriate from around age 5, while a couple of other outlets suggest 6 or 7 as a more comfortable floor given the thematic weight. Most school-age children will be completely fine with it.

Why it's rated PG

Themes
Screen addiction and being replaced

The film's emotional core deals with modern themes — digital addiction, changing childhood habits, and toys' fear of becoming obsolete. Reviewers consistently note this is handled thoughtfully rather than heavy-handedly, and several point out it opens a genuinely useful conversation about screen time with kids.

Peril
Mild suspense, no graphic violence

Expect toys falling or being thrown from heights, fast-moving chase sequences, and a chaotic set piece involving multiple Buzz Lightyears navigating rafts. Reviewers describe the tension as brief and appropriate for a PG audience — nothing genuinely frightening, more suspenseful than scary.

Humor
Rude humor, mild throughout

The "rude humor" in the rating refers to mild potty humor and playful name-calling typical of the franchise — reviewers note some conversations about toileting and a bit of name-calling, alongside a couple of kisses and light discussion of proposing marriage.

Other
No violence, sex, or strong language

Multiple sources confirm no graphic violence, no sexual content, and no strong profanity anywhere in the film — language is limited to mild insults and playful quips.

What the film is about

The spotlight shifts to Jessie (voiced by Joan Cusack), who becomes the main toy of Bonnie — now eight years old — as she navigates a childhood increasingly mediated by technology. A new character, Lilypad (voiced by Anna Faris), represents an addictive electronic tablet that requires supervision, and the toys' central conflict becomes less about a rival toy and more about competing with a screen for a child's attention.

Reviewers note the film resists an easy "technology is evil" message, instead offering what one review called a nuanced cautionary tale about tech use in childhood — and a natural jumping-off point for parents to talk with their kids about screen time, which pairs neatly with the film's own subject matter.

Age-by-age guidance

Under 5

Reviewers generally suggest younger children may need a parent nearby during the more suspenseful chase sequences, though nothing in the film is genuinely frightening. The emotional themes about being forgotten or replaced may be more abstract than concerning for this age.

5–8

The range most reviews converge on as the comfortable core audience. Kids at this age will follow the story easily and are old enough to engage — lightly — with its themes about screen time without being overwhelmed.

9 and up

Older kids, especially those already negotiating their own screen time with parents, may find the film's themes land with unexpected relevance. Pixar's tradition of hiding depth for older viewers inside a kid-friendly story is fully present here.

Worth knowing

One reviewer specifically flagged that Pixar's PG ratings can undersell intensity — pointing to Toy Story 3's furnace scene as a prior example of a "PG" moment that hit much harder than the rating implied. Toy Story 5 doesn't appear to have an equivalent moment based on current reviews, but it's a reasonable reminder to preview if your child is especially sensitive.

Frequently asked questions

Is Toy Story 5 appropriate for kids?

Yes, for most school-age children. It's rated PG for thematic elements and rude humor, with no graphic violence, sexual content, or strong language. Common Sense Media suggests it works from around age 5, with younger children possibly needing a parent nearby during suspenseful moments.

What is Toy Story 5 rated and why?

PG, for some thematic elements and rude humor. The rating is driven almost entirely by the film's emotional themes — screen addiction, being replaced, changing childhood habits — rather than by violence or language.

Is Toy Story 5 scary for young kids?

Not genuinely scary. Reviewers describe brief suspenseful chase sequences and toys facing danger from heights, but nothing that crosses into frightening territory for most children.

What is Toy Story 5 about?

Jessie becomes Bonnie's main toy as the group faces a new kind of rival: an addictive tablet named Lilypad. The film explores themes of screen time, digital addiction, and the fear of becoming obsolete, wrapped in the franchise's usual humor and heart.

Where can I watch Toy Story 5?

It released in theaters June 19, 2026, via Walt Disney Studios. A Disney+ streaming release is expected to follow after the theatrical window.
